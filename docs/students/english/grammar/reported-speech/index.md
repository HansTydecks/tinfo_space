# Reported speech <Badge type="tip" text="indirect speech" />

## Goals
- report what somebody **said**, **asked** or **told** you to do
- move the tense **one step back** (backshift)
- change **pronouns**, **time words** and **place words**
- use **say** and **tell** correctly

**Reported speech** is used when you tell someone **what another person said**.
*Mit der indirekten Rede gibst du wieder, was jemand anderes gesagt hat.*

## What changes? The big picture {#big-picture}

/>

The colours are the same in every picture on this page:

| colour | what changes | example |
|---|---|---|
| 🟦 **blue** | **pronouns** – *who* is meant | I → **he**, my → **his** |
| 🟥 **pink** | **tense** – one step back (*backshift*) | am → **was**, will → **would** |
| 🟩 **green** | **time and place words** | today → **that day**, here → **there** |
| 🟧 **orange** | **word order** in questions and commands | Are you …? → **if I was** … |

::: tip The 4 questions for every sentence
1. **Who** is speaking now – and to whom? → change the **pronouns**
2. **When** was it said? Is the reporting verb in the past (*said*)? → **backshift**
3. **When and where** is it now? → change **time and place words**
4. **What kind** of sentence is it? statement · question · command → choose the right **pattern**
:::

## 1 · Backshift – one step back in time {#backshift}

If the reporting verb is in the **past** (*said, told, asked …*), the tense moves **one step back into the past**.
*Steht das einleitende Verb in der Vergangenheit, rückt die Zeitform eine Stufe zurück.*

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="backshift"
  caption="Only the first verb changes: am → was, have → had, will → would. Present perfect and past simple both become past perfect."
/>

| direct speech | → | reported speech |
|---|:-:|---|
| **present simple**<br>“I **play** football.” | → | **past simple**<br>He said he **played** football. |
| **present progressive**<br>“I **am watching** TV.” | → | **past progressive**<br>She said she **was watching** TV. |
| **present perfect**<br>“I **have finished**.” | → | **past perfect**<br>He said he **had finished**. |
| **past simple**<br>“I **saw** a fox.” | → | **past perfect**<br>She said she **had seen** a fox. |
| **past progressive**<br>“I **was sleeping**.” | → | **past perfect progressive**<br>He said he **had been sleeping**. |
| **will-future**<br>“I **will help** you.” | → | **would**<br>She said she **would help** me. |
| **going to**<br>“I **am going to** call.” | → | **was going to**<br>He said he **was going to** call. |
| **past perfect**<br>“I **had** never **seen** it.” | → | **past perfect** (no change)<br>She said she **had** never **seen** it. |

### Modal verbs {#modal-verbs}

| direct | → | reported | example |
|---|:-:|---|---|
| **can** | → | **could** | “I **can** swim.” → He said he **could** swim. |
| **may** | → | **might** | “It **may** rain.” → She said it **might** rain. |
| **must** | → | **had to** (or *must*) | “I **must** go.” → He said he **had to** go. |
| **shall** (question) | → | **should** | “**Shall** I help?” → She asked if she **should** help. |
| **would, could, should, might, ought to** | → | **no change** | “You **should** rest.” → He said I **should** rest. |

::: tip Trick: only move the FIRST verb
Look at the **first** verb of the sentence and put it **one step into the past**:
*am* working → *was* working · *have* worked → *had* worked · *will* work → *would* work · *worked* → *had* worked
:::

### When is there no backshift? {#no-backshift}

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="backshift-check"
  caption="No backshift when the reporting verb is in the present. When something is still true, backshift is possible but not necessary."
/>

- **Reporting verb in the present**: *Tom **says** he **is** tired.* – nothing changes in the tense.
- **Still true or a general fact**: *Our teacher said the Earth **goes** / **went** round the sun.*

## 2 · Pronouns – who is speaking now? {#pronouns}

Pronouns always depend on **who is speaking now** and **who is listening**.
*Die Pronomen hängen davon ab, wer jetzt spricht – nicht davon, wer es ursprünglich gesagt hat.*

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="pronouns"
  :min-width="400"
  caption="Tom’s sentence “I like you” is reported in three different ways. “I” always means Tom, “you” always means Anna – but the words change with the speaker."
/>

| direct | → | reported (Tom said …) | example |
|---|:-:|---|---|
| **I** | → | **he / she** | “**I** am hungry.” → Tom said **he** was hungry. |
| **me** | → | **him / her** | “Help **me**!” → She asked us to help **her**. |
| **my** · **mine** | → | **his / her** · **his / hers** | “It’s **my** bag.” → He said it was **his** bag. |
| **we** · **us** | → | **they** · **them** | “**We** are late.” → They said **they** were late. |
| **our** · **ours** | → | **their** · **theirs** | “**Our** dog is ill.” → They said **their** dog was ill. |
| **you** (to me) | → | **I / me** | “**You** are nice.” → She said **I** was nice. |
| **your** (to me) | → | **my** | “Where is **your** pen?” → He asked where **my** pen was. |
| **you** (to someone else) | → | **he / she / they** | “**You** are late, Ben.” → She told Ben **he** was late. |

::: warning Think about the people – not about a rule
There is no fixed rule for **you**: ask yourself **who** is meant and who is speaking now.
*“You” wird zu I, he, she oder they – je nachdem, wer gemeint ist.*
:::

## 3 · Time and place words {#time-place}

When you report something **later** or **somewhere else**, words like *today*, *tomorrow* or *here* do not fit any more.

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="time"
  caption="Tom’s “tomorrow” is Tuesday. If Anna said “tomorrow” on Friday, it would mean Saturday – so she says “the next day”."
/>

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="place"
  caption="“Here” is always where the speaker is. Anna is at school, not at the beach – so “here” becomes “there”."
/>

| direct | → | reported |
|---|:-:|---|
| **now** | → | **then** / at that moment |
| **today** | → | **that day** |
| **tonight** | → | **that night** |
| **yesterday** | → | **the day before** / the previous day |
| **tomorrow** | → | **the next day** / the following day |
| **last week** / last year | → | **the week before** / the previous week |
| **next week** / next year | → | **the week after** / the following week |
| **two days ago** | → | **two days before** |
| **this** (week, morning …) | → | **that** (week, morning …) |
| **these** | → | **those** |
| **here** | → | **there** |
| **come** (here) | → | **go** (there) |
| **bring** | → | **take** |

::: tip Only change what really changed
If Anna reports Tom’s words **on the same day**, *today* stays *today*. If you are still **in the same place**, *here* stays *here*.
:::

## 4 · say or tell? Reporting verbs and their prepositions {#say-tell}

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="say-tell"
  caption="say: the person is optional and needs “to”. tell: the person is a must – and there is no “to”."
/>

| verb | pattern | example |
|---|---|---|
| **say** | say (that) … · say **to** sb | She **said** (**to** me) that she was tired. |
| **tell** | tell **sb** (that) … | She **told me** that she was tired. |
| **ask** | ask (**sb**) if / whether / wh- … | He **asked me** where I lived. |
| **explain** | explain (**to** sb) that … | She **explained to us** that the bus was late. |
| **reply** · **answer** | reply (**to** sb) that … · answer that … | He **replied** that he didn’t know. |
| **promise** | promise (sb) **to** do / that … | Mum **promised to** call. |
| **apologise** | apologise (**to** sb) **for** -ing | He **apologised for** being late. |
| **complain** | complain (**to** sb) **about** sth | She **complained about** the noise. |
| **advise** · **warn** | advise / warn **sb** (not) **to** do | The doctor **advised me to** stay in bed. |
| **remind** | remind **sb to** do | He **reminded me to** take my keys. |

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ She **said me** that … → ✓ She **told me** that … / She **said to me** that …
✗ She **told to me** … → ✓ She **told me** …
✗ He **explained me** the rules. → ✓ He **explained** the rules **to me**.
:::

## 5 · Questions {#questions}

Reported questions are **not real questions any more**:
- **no** *do / does / did*
- **word order** like in a normal sentence: subject + verb
- **no question mark**
- yes/no questions get **if** or **whether**

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="questions"
  caption="In reported questions the subject comes before the verb – just like in a normal statement."
/>

| type | direct | reported |
|---|---|---|
| **wh-question** | “**Where do you live**?” | She asked me **where I lived**. |
| | “**What are you doing**?” | He asked **what I was doing**. |
| | “**Why did you leave**?” | She asked **why I had left**. |
| **yes / no question** | “**Are you** hungry?” | He asked **if I was** hungry. |
| | “**Can you** swim?” | She asked **whether I could** swim. |
| | “**Do you like** pizza?” | He asked **if I liked** pizza. |

::: danger Word order!
✗ She asked me where **did I live**. → ✓ She asked me where **I lived**.
✗ He asked **was I** hungry. → ✓ He asked **if I was** hungry.
:::

## 6 · Commands and requests {#commands}

For commands and requests you **don’t** need backshift – you use **to + infinitive**.

<ReportedSpeechGraphic
  name="commands"
  caption="Pattern: told / asked + person + (not) to + verb. “please” is a sign for “asked”."
/>

| direct | reported |
|---|---|
| “**Sit down**!” | The teacher **told us to sit** down. |
| “**Please help** me!” | She **asked me to help** her. |
| “**Don’t be** late!” | Mum **told me not to be** late. |
| “**Don’t touch** it, Ben!” | I **told Ben not to touch** it. |
| “**Could you open** the window, please?” | He **asked me to open** the window. |

## Everything at a glance {#everything-at-a-glance}

| | direct speech | reported speech |
|---|---|---|
| **statement** | Tom: “I **am** tired **today**.” | Tom **said (that)** he **was** tired **that day**. |
| | Tom: “I**’ll** call you **tomorrow**.” | Tom **told me (that)** he **would** call me **the next day**. |
| **wh-question** | Lisa: “Where **do you live**?” | Lisa **asked me where I lived**. |
| **yes/no question** | Lisa: “**Are you** OK?” | Lisa **asked if I was** OK. |
| **command** | Mum: “**Tidy** your room!” | Mum **told me to tidy** my room. |
| **negative command** | Mum: “**Don’t** shout!” | Mum **told us not to** shout. |
| **request** | Ben: “Can you help me, **please**?” | Ben **asked me to help** him. |

::: tip Step by step: from direct to reported speech
1. Choose the **reporting verb**: *said · told sb · asked sb*
2. Change the **pronouns** – who is speaking now?
3. **Backshift** – move the first verb one step back
4. Change **time and place words** – if the time or place is different now
5. Questions: **if / whether**, normal word order · Commands: **(not) to** + verb
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Put the sentences into reported speech. Then open the solution.

1. Sam: “I **am** hungry.” → Sam said …
2. Mia: “I **have lost** my phone.” → Mia said …
3. Leo: “I **will** come **tomorrow**.” → Leo said …
4. Mr Brown: “**Where do** you **live**?” → Mr Brown asked me …
5. Emma: “**Do** you **like** cats?” → Emma asked me …
6. The teacher: “**Don’t** talk!” → The teacher told us …

::: details Solutions
1. Sam said (that) he **was** hungry.
2. Mia said (that) she **had lost her** phone.
3. Leo said (that) he **would** come **the next day**.
4. Mr Brown asked me **where I lived**.
5. Emma asked me **if / whether I liked** cats.
6. The teacher told us **not to talk**.
:::
