# Prepositions of manner

## Goals
- say **how** you travel: *by bus, by train, on foot*
- use fixed phrases like *by email, in English, on purpose, in a hurry*
- tell the difference between **like** and **as**

Prepositions of manner answer the question **How?** – *Wie / auf welche Art und Weise?*

## How do you travel?

| | examples |
|---|---|
| **by** + transport (no *a*, *the*, *my*!) | **by** bus, **by** car, **by** train, **by** plane, **by** bike, **by** boat, **by** taxi |
| **on** foot | I go to school **on** foot. (*zu Fuß*) |
| **in** + *my / the / a* car or taxi | I came **in** my dad’s car. – We went **in** a taxi. |
| **on** + *my / the / a* bus, train, bike | She goes to work **on** her bike. – I met him **on** the train. |

::: warning by car – but in my car
As soon as there is *a, the, my, his …*, you **can’t** use *by*:
~~by my car~~ → **in** my car · ~~by the bus~~ → **on** the bus
:::

## Fixed phrases

These are best learned as **chunks**:

| **by** | **in** | **on** |
|---|---|---|
| **by** email, **by** post | **in** English, **in** German | **on** purpose (*absichtlich*) |
| **by** phone (or **on** the phone) | **in** pen, **in** pencil | **on** the phone |
| **by** heart (*auswendig*) | **in** cash (*bar*) | **on** the radio, **on** TV |
| **by** hand (*von Hand*) | **in** a hurry (*in Eile*) | **on** foot |
| **by** mistake, **by** accident (*aus Versehen*) | **in** silence, **in** a quiet voice | |
| pay **by** card | **in** alphabetical order | |

::: danger Typical mistake
*absichtlich* = **on** purpose (~~by purpose~~) · *aus Versehen* = **by** accident / **by** mistake
:::

## with, without, like, as

| | Deutsch | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **with** | mit | She answered **with** a smile. – Carry it **with** care. |
| **without** | ohne | He left **without** saying goodbye. |
| **like** | wie (similar, but not really) | He sings **like** a bird. – Don’t behave **like** a baby! |
| **as** | als (that’s the job or role) | My mum works **as** a nurse. – I used my jacket **as** a pillow. |

::: tip like or as?
*She works **as** a teacher.* → She **is** a teacher. 👩‍🏫
*She talks **like** a teacher.* → She is **not** a teacher, she only sounds like one.
:::
