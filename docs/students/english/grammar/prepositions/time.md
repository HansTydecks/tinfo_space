# Prepositions of time

## Goals
- use **at**, **on** and **in** for times, days, months and years
- know when you need **no preposition** (*next week*, *tomorrow*)
- use **for**, **since**, **until**, **by** and **during** correctly

Prepositions of time answer the question **When?** or **How long?**
*Präpositionen der Zeit beantworten die Frage* **Wann?** *oder* **Wie lange?**

## at – on – in

Think of a pyramid: from **small** (an exact time) to **big** (a year).

| | used with | examples | Deutsch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **at** | clock times, exact points | **at** 7 o’clock, **at** half past six, **at** noon, **at** midnight, **at** night | um 7 Uhr, mittags, nachts |
| **on** | days and dates | **on** Monday, **on** Fridays, **on** 12 May, **on** my birthday, **on** Christmas Day | am Montag, am 12. Mai |
| **in** | months, years, seasons, longer periods | **in** July, **in** 2015, **in** winter, **in** the 1990s, **in** the 21st century | im Juli, 2015, im Winter |

::: tip Memory trick
**at** = a **point** on the clock ⏰ · **on** = a **day** in the calendar 📅 · **in** = a **long time** (month, year) 🗓️
:::

Examples:
- The film starts **at** 8 o’clock.
- We have PE **on** Tuesday.
- My birthday is **in** May. – My birthday is **on** 12 May.
- I was born **in** 2012.

### Parts of the day

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **in** the morning | **in** the afternoon | **in** the evening |
| **at** night | **at** noon / midday | **at** midnight |
| **on** Monday morning | **on** Friday evening | **on** Sunday afternoon |

::: warning Careful
*in the morning* – but *on Monday morning*. As soon as a **day** is there, you use **on**.
:::

### Holidays and the weekend
- **at** Christmas, **at** Easter, **at** the weekend (British English) – *on the weekend* (American English)
- **on** Christmas Day, **on** New Year’s Eve, **on** my birthday (a single day → **on**)

## No preposition

Before **next, last, this, every** and before **today, tomorrow, yesterday, tonight** there is **no preposition**.

| ✅ right | ❌ wrong |
|---|---|
| See you **next week**. | ~~See you on next week.~~ |
| I went there **last Friday**. | ~~I went there on last Friday.~~ |
| I’m busy **this afternoon**. | ~~I’m busy in this afternoon.~~ |
| We play tennis **every Sunday**. | ~~We play tennis on every Sunday.~~ |
| I’ll do it **tomorrow**. | ~~I’ll do it at tomorrow.~~ |

## for, since, until, by, during

| | meaning | examples | Deutsch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **for** | a length of time | **for** ten years, **for** two hours, **for** a long time | seit / für (Zeitdauer) |
| **since** | a starting point | **since** 2015, **since** Monday, **since** I was six | seit (Zeitpunkt) |
| **until** | up to a point in time | The shop is open **until** 8 pm. | bis |
| **by** | at the latest (deadline) | Hand in your homework **by** Friday. | bis spätestens |
| **during** | while something happens | I fell asleep **during** the film. | während |

::: danger Typical mistake: „seit"
German *seit* can be **for** or **since** in English!
- *Ich wohne seit zehn Jahren hier.* → I have lived here **for** ten years. (Dauer)
- *Ich wohne seit 2015 hier.* → I have lived here **since** 2015. (Startpunkt)

Use the **present perfect** with *for* and *since*: ~~I live here since 2015.~~ → I **have lived** here since 2015.
:::

::: details until or by?
- **until** = something **continues** up to that time: *I’ll wait **until** 5.* (I wait the whole time.)
- **by** = something happens **before or at** that time at the latest: *Be home **by** 9!* (Not later than 9.)
:::
