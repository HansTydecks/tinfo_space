# Prepositions of source

## Goals
- say **where** someone or something **comes from**: *from*
- tell the difference between **made of** and **made from**
- use **by** for the person who made a song, book or film, and **out of** for things that come from inside

Prepositions of source answer the question **Where from?** – *Woher kommt etwas?*

<EnglishSilentTeacherButton kapitel="source" />

## from

| | examples |
|---|---|
| origin | I come **from** Germany. – Where are you **from**? – The coffee comes **from** Brazil. |
| the person who gives or sends | a letter **from** my aunt – a present **from** my grandma – an email **from** our teacher |
| a place you take something from | I borrowed this book **from** the library. |

## made of or made from?

| | rule | examples |
|---|---|---|
| made **of** | you can **still see** the material | This table is made **of** wood. – Her ring is made **of** gold. |
| made **from** | the material has **changed** | Paper is made **from** wood. – Cheese is made **from** milk. |

::: tip Test question
Can you still see the material? A wooden table is clearly wood → **of**.
You can’t see wood in paper anymore → **from**.
:::

## from, by or out of?

| | when? | example |
|---|---|---|
| **from** | who gave or sent it | This is a present **from** my parents. |
| **by** | who **made** it (book, song, film, painting) | I love this song **by** Billie Eilish. – a book **by** Roald Dahl |
| **out of** | it comes from **inside** something | She took her phone **out of** her bag. |

::: danger Typical mistake: „von"
*ein Lied von Adele* → a song **by** Adele (~~of Adele~~) · *ein Geschenk von Oma* → a present **from** Grandma
:::
