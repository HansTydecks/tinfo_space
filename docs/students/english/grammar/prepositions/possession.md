# Prepositions of possession

## Goals
- use **of** for parts and things that belong together: *the end **of** the film*
- use **with** and **without** to say what someone or something has (or hasn’t)
- use **belong to** and **a friend of mine**

Prepositions of possession answer the question **Whose? What does it have?** – *Wem gehört es? Was hat es?*

## of

| | examples |
|---|---|
| a part of something | the end **of** the film – the top **of** the mountain – the roof **of** the house |
| it belongs to something | the capital **of** France – the king **of** Spain – the name **of** the song |
| a member | a member **of** our football club – the leader **of** the group |

::: info 's or of?
For **people and animals** we usually use **’s**: *my brother’s bike*, *the dog’s bed*.
For **things** we usually use **of**: *the end **of** the film* (*the film’s end* is possible, but less common).
:::

## with and without

| | Deutsch | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **with** | mit (it has) | a girl **with** long hair – a house **with** a big garden – tea **with** milk |
| **without** | ohne (it hasn’t) | coffee **without** milk – a room **without** windows |

## belong to, a friend of mine

| | examples |
|---|---|
| **belong to** (*gehören*) | This bike belongs **to** my brother. – Does this phone belong **to** you? |
| **a … of mine / yours / Lisa’s** | She’s a friend **of** mine. (*eine Freundin von mir*) – Is he a friend **of** yours? |

::: danger Typical mistake
*Das Fahrrad gehört mir.* → The bike belongs **to** me. (~~belongs me~~)
*eine Freundin von mir* → a friend **of mine** (~~a friend of me~~)
:::
